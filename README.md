## Your first week in Tokyo

Three useful first steps:

1. **Find your people.** Start with [TokyoDev](https://www.tokyodev.com/events) for international developers or [HN Tokyo](https://hntokyo.doorkeeper.jp/) for founders and technologists. Choose an event with a confirmed Tokyo venue and RSVP.
2. **Pick one startup gathering.** Look at [Venture Café Tokyo](https://venturecafetokyo.org/) and select a session whose language and admission suit you. Check the dated event listing before travelling.
3. **Choose a base around your actual plans.** Shibuya for west-side meetings; Shimbashi/Toranomon for startup events; Yurakucho/Ginza for Tokyo Innovation Base. These are editorial planning suggestions, not measured travel-time comparisons.

For a short visit, book one technical meetup and one founder event before filling the rest of your calendar. For a longer stay, return to the same community and offer to help.

**How to read access information:** *English* means the organizer explicitly describes an English-speaking community. *Mixed / event-specific* means you must inspect the session language. *English information* means an English official page exists; it does not guarantee English-speaking staff. RSVP, membership, and booking are separate requirements.

**Google for Startups Campus Tokyo — verify access first.** The Shibuya campus is a relevant lead, but its [former official campus URL](https://startup.google.com/campus/tokyo/) redirected to Google's general startup homepage when checked. Current visitor admission and membership availability could not be confirmed. Use a current organizer-issued invitation or event registration with a confirmed Tokyo address; do not plan on walk-in coworking. This is an unconfirmed-access lead, not a verified public workspace.

## Cafes and places to work

These serve different needs. A workspace booking does not guarantee networking opportunities.

| Place | Area | Good for | Access and practical notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [FabCafe Tokyo](https://fabcafe.com/jp/tokyo/) | Dogenzaka, Shibuya | Coffee and a casual meeting in a creative setting. | Public cafe; buy food or a drink. Ask about laptop use and available outlets before settling in. Event admission is separate. Source in Japanese. |
| [Fuglen Tokyo](https://fuglencoffee.jp/pages/fuglen-tokyo) | Tomigaya, Shibuya | Nordic-roast coffee and a casual conversation; cocktails at night. | Public cafe/bar; order a drink. English information on the official page. Wi-Fi, outlets, and extended laptop use are not verified. |
| [.andwork Shibuya](https://www.livelyhotels.com/en/themillennialsshibuya/faq/) | Shibuya | A work session with Wi-Fi and power. | Non-guests can use hourly plans after free registration; walk-ins depend on seats. The official FAQ lists Wi-Fi and outlets at most seats. English information. |
| [SHARE LOUNGE, Shibuya Scramble Square](https://www.sharelounge.jp/store/2330) | Shibuya station, 11F | A paid lounge for focused work between meetings. | Timed paid access. Official amenities include Wi-Fi, power, and drinks. Check capacity and rates. Source in Japanese. |
| [BasisPoint Shimbashi Ginza-guchi](https://basispoint.tokyo/store/coworking/shimbashi/) | Shimbashi, Minato | Coworking before a Toranomon-area event. | Paid drop-in; official page lists Wi-Fi and power at open seats. Calls and conversations are permitted, so expect some noise. Source in Japanese. |

For a quiet call, reserve a suitable booth or room and check its rules. For a coffee meeting, avoid assuming that a cafe permits long laptop sessions.

## Find your next event

- [TokyoDev events](https://www.tokyodev.com/events): English-friendly discovery. Select a physical Tokyo venue; the broader calendar is not Tokyo-only.
- [HN Tokyo](https://hntokyo.doorkeeper.jp/): organizer listings and registration.
- [Venture Café Tokyo](https://venturecafetokyo.org/): check the program, language, venue, and admission for the date you want.
- [Tokyo AI](https://www.tokyoai.jp/): follow its official links for AI gatherings.

Prefer an upcoming, dated organizer page over a recurring schedule copied into a blog. Check the address, start time, language, ticket status, and building-entry instructions. All local event times should be read in **Japan Standard Time (UTC+9)** unless the organizer says otherwise.
