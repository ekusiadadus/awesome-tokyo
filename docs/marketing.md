# Launch plan

Research date: 2026-09-24. This is a proposed launch strategy; channel performance has not been measured.

## Posting check: stopped

On 2026-09-24, we checked the leading candidate before posting. **TokyoDev prohibits advertising**, so we stopped. Its [official Discord invitation](https://discord.com/invite/seUNe38YgV), linked from [TokyoDev](https://www.tokyodev.com/events), showed 8,970 members. The membership application displayed Rule 5: “No advertising of any sort.” The application was not submitted and no message was posted.

[r/JapanDev](https://www.reddit.com/r/JapanDev/) also prohibits traffic-driven promotion and AI-generated posts/comments. We did not post there. Its live sidebar showed 4.6K weekly visitors; this is a different measure from Discord membership, so it does not establish which audience is larger.

TokyoDev remains a useful resource for readers, but it is **excluded as a promotional posting channel**. The earlier ranking reflected audience fit before internal rules were checked. No permitted largest channel has been established. Tokyo AI and HN Tokyo still need their own posting-rule checks; we have not moved the campaign there.

The promise: **Your first week in Tokyo as a developer or founder—with places to meet people, work, and stay.**

Lead with entry requirements and English access. These help a newcomer take action. GitHub is the home for the guide and community corrections.

## Structure built for newcomers

1. Your first week in Tokyo.
2. Choose your neighborhood: Marunouchi/Yurakucho, Shibuya, or Toranomon/Shimbashi.
3. Meet developers and founders.
4. If you speak Japanese: specialist communities and how to join.
5. Startup hubs and coworking.
6. Cafes and places to work.
7. Where to stay.
8. Find your next event.
9. Contribute and keep it current.

## More useful ways to share

- For a visitor staying near Tokyo Station: link directly to the Marunouchi/Yurakucho guide.
- For a founder based in Shibuya: share the station, Dogenzaka/Tomigaya, and Shibuya Bridge options.
- For a Japanese-speaking developer: share the technical-community section, starting with their language or tool.

Groups such as vim-jp are communities to participate in, not assumed advertising channels. Their inclusion does not establish permission to promote this guide there.

## Proposed next steps — subject to channel rules

- Week 1: GitHub publication is complete. TokyoDev and r/JapanDev are excluded. Check any new candidate's rules before choosing a place to share.
- Week 2: Apply feedback. Explore whether local organizers find the guide useful for new attendees.
- Week 3: Help people asking relevant questions, with two or three concrete suggestions. Avoid link-only promotion.
- Week 4: Compare useful feedback and available referral data. Choose the next channel based on evidence.

Track GitHub referral sources and unique visitors where available, outside issues/PRs, and voluntary reports of successful visits. Stars are secondary. Record traffic weekly because GitHub retains traffic data for a limited period. Do not treat unattributed visits as known channel conversions.

A proposed first-month target: five useful corrections and three reports that the guide helped someone attend. No results have been measured yet.
